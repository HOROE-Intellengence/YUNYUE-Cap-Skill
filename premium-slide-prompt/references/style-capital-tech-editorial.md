# Institutional Capital-Tech Editorial

Use this as the default mother style for investor decks unless explicit brand assets or a user-supplied style direction establish a stronger identity.

The personality is **institutional capital judgment × editorial information order × selective industry materiality**. Make the deck feel designed by an investor-facing editorial team with access to real products and technical evidence—not by a generic technology template.

## Core character

- Let investment judgment and evidence determine visual hierarchy.
- Use typography, numbers, tables, diagrams, and real proof as the deck's backbone.
- Introduce product, technical, or scene imagery selectively to explain, verify, or create a memory peak.
- Maintain a disciplined institutional frame while allowing industry-specific color, material, and photography.
- Prefer clear commercial communication over minimalism for its own sake.

## Information order

Use this visual reading pattern:

`investment judgment -> one primary number, object, or diagram -> 2–3 evidence groups -> optional new implication -> source`

Write headlines as conclusions rather than topic labels. Build one complete central structure instead of scattering many unrelated widgets.

Use containers only when they clarify evidence grouping. Permit no-container layouts, open tables, editorial columns, and full-width diagrams.

## Color behavior

Derive color in this order:

1. explicit user or brand guidelines;
2. consistent colors observable across supplied assets;
3. industry-relevant semantic colors;
4. neutral institutional defaults.

Use one primary hue family and usually one semantic accent. Reserve additional colors for status or comparison only when necessary.

Use semantic roles consistently:

- primary hue: company, system, or current capability;
- green or equivalent positive accent: validated progress, health, efficiency, or commercial proof when brand-compatible;
- amber, gold, or orange: opportunity, milestone, certification, or future stage;
- red: verified risk, exception, or warning only;
- neutral gray: context, benchmark, or de-emphasized evidence.

Do not force blue, cyan, and gold simply because the subject is technology or capital.

## Material behavior

- Use bright or softly tinted canvases for most evidence pages.
- Use dark or immersive canvases selectively for covers, section turns, or terminal vision.
- Prefer fine rules, low-opacity fills, shallow shadows, and controlled contrast.
- Use porcelain, metal, glass, photographic, or diagrammatic material according to the evidence carrier rather than applying one material to the whole deck.
- Keep glow localized and purposeful.

Do not default to glass cards, glowing pedestals, engineering grids, circuit patterns, or ambient waves.

## Image behavior

Assign every image one role:

- **Evidence:** product, certificate, result, customer deployment, or real operating scene.
- **Explanation:** cutaway, topology, process, layer stack, or mechanism.
- **Memory:** one distinctive product, scene, contrast, or physical metaphor.

Remove imagery that has no role. Treat generic category images as low-priority accompaniment and keep them subordinate.

## Deck composition

Aim for roughly:

- 60–80% Quiet or Standard evidence-led pages;
- selective Scientific Mechanism and Commercial Momentum pages according to content;
- 2–4 Strategic Hero peaks in a normal investor deck;
- Corporate Ceremony only when the page function explicitly requires it.

Keep typography, grid, headline geometry, source treatment, and semantic color roles stable while allowing page density within the attention budget, image share, background value, and material expression to change.

## Reference-derived traits to preserve

Preserve these useful traits from strong investor-slide references:

- conclusion-led titles with strong numeric identity;
- coherent central diagrams or comparisons;
- evidence cards with different internal grammars;
- real product and industry imagery used as proof or strategic contrast;
- stage-based growth, market, and commercialization visualizations;
- restrained bottom verdict bands and readable sources.

Reject these traits when generalized:

- identical white-blue glass cards on every page;
- a glowing platform under every object;
- decorative engineering grids and light waves on all canvases;
- medals, laurels, shields, or “global first” badges standing in for proof;
- every process rendered as a circular track;
- continuous Hero intensity across the whole deck.
