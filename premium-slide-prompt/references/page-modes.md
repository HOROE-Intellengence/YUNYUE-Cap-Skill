# Page Modes

Apply one primary structural grammar per slide. Use a secondary mode only when the content genuinely requires it and keep one mode visibly dominant.

## Investor-cover

Communicate project level, financing stage, and strongest credibility within three seconds.

Use a split composition as the default starting point:

- reserve the left or center-left for the investment-defining headline and capital labels;
- place one product, device, or metaphorical object on the right;
- scale the object according to its communication role;
- allow midpoint crossing or full-bleed treatment only when the headline and labels remain fully readable.

Apart from the headline and a restrained company name or Logo, allow 2–4 short capital labels, including the financing round:

1. financing round — mandatory;
2. specific industry track — only when distinctive;
3. strong financial metric — revenue, profit, orders, installed base, or scale with period and unit;
4. team credibility — recognizable company heritage or meaningful industry tenure;
5. verified customer or capital endorsement.

Do not force all five categories. Omit weak labels. Never invent a missing financing round, metric, affiliation, relationship, or endorsement; retain an explicit placeholder when needed.

Optionally add one concise capital-credential band combining 2–3 short hooks from team credibility, orders, or verified capital endorsement. Never turn it into a company introduction, product description, mission statement, feature list, or paragraph.

Let the right-side object occupy half or more of the canvas only on a visual-led cover with protected title readability. Use a full-slide hero render only when the content and chosen style support it.

## Evidence-portfolio

Use a conventional three-column system as the default when a slide must establish several distinct investment reasons at once. Use four containers only when four parallel categories are essential. Create sophistication through selection, hierarchy, and internal variation rather than an exotic layout.

Apply **same shell, different evidence grammar**:

- keep outer geometry, numbering, title baseline, corner treatment, and gutters consistent;
- let each container use the evidence form best suited to its claim: an oversized market number, a product or technology assertion with proof strips, a compact growth chart, or a team capability equation;
- do not repeat the same “large number + two lines” template in every container;
- order investment reasons as `market opportunity -> technical right to win -> commercial proof -> team ability to deliver` when that sequence fits the evidence.

Use up to three internal zones per container:

1. small index or category marker;
2. short claim title;
3. one concise evidence expression;

Optionally add one compact proof or status line when the evidence expression needs qualification. Do not force all zones to appear. If a claim cannot be understood with one evidence expression and one proof line, give it a dedicated slide.

Reserve the top band for the investment conclusion. Use one supported exceptional outcome, forecast, or verdict as the slide-level accent. Build controlled asymmetry through different internal evidence forms, not through arbitrary container geometry.

Use contrast semantically and highlight only evidence that materially changes the investment judgment. Use icons only as evidence labels. Let the selected visual expression, brand DNA, and material budget determine color, imagery, containers, and depth.

## Investment-highlight-grid

Use exactly four cards in this order unless the supplied outline has a stronger investment logic:

`market opportunity -> technical right to win -> commercial proof -> team ability to deliver`

- give each card one controlled hook: one primary number or one primary assertion;
- allow equal weight or make the market card slightly stronger;
- unify the slide with one conclusion headline, consistent shells, aligned baselines, and deliberate reading order;
- vary the internal evidence grammar instead of repeating one template four times;
- keep at most one proof-bearing line or one status line per card;
- retain a weakly supported category and mark the evidence gap instead of filling it with generic copy;
- never add nested subcards, feature lists, multiple icons, or a second focal number, assertion, or illustration inside a card.

## Argument-map

Use this mode for a competition map, core-strength directory, or section overview that prepares later proof pages.

- allow up to five parallel, equal-weight entries;
- use numbering or concise icons only when they accelerate scanning; do not require both;
- state each strength's investment meaning rather than its complete proof;
- map entries to later proof pages when the outline supplies that sequence;
- keep the page visibly navigational instead of presenting unverified claims as completed evidence;
- never force each entry to prove itself on the same slide.

## Conclusion-number

Let one number, equation, waterfall, or quantified chain dominate. Use restrained separators and short labels. Make the result box or final verdict visually decisive.

For a rhetorical equation with a qualitative verdict on the right, use multiplication signs to communicate compounding factors without implying a calculated numeric result. Permit cross-dimensional factors in this qualitative form. Never add a numeric output unless units and calculation reconcile.

Keep CAGR, growth multiples, market shares, axes, and other conventional quantitative results mathematically self-consistent across the slide and deck.

## Rigorous-comparison

Use a readable table or aligned competitor columns. Show only 3–5 decision-relevant criteria. Highlight the project column and 1–3 decisive differences while keeping other entries neutral. Repeat identical internal layouts when exact comparison benefits. Add application proof only when it verifies the comparison. Move exhaustive specifications to appendix or DD. Finish with one investor-facing conclusion.

## Capability-progression

Show three chronological stages by default; use four only when the fourth is essential to the investment conclusion. Make each stage add one capability while visibly inheriting the prior foundation.

Give every stage only:

- timeframe;
- one capability gained;
- one representative product, metric, or proof;
- one short inheritance cue when continuity would otherwise be unclear.

Do not show complete product portfolios, software feature inventories, customer-geography maps, and service capabilities inside every stage. Select one representative proof per stage and let the visual spine communicate accumulation.

Make the progression read as:

`technical foundation -> product/equipment realization -> capability expansion -> platform or standard`

Use one shared visual spine only when the evidence proves continuity. Let the selected visual expression determine whether that spine appears as a rule, timeline, platform, object sequence, optical beam, or data stream.

If an external benchmark is necessary, reduce it to one compact precedent strip containing no more than one scale metric and one terminal-state lesson. Do not combine a full benchmark dashboard with a full company roadmap on the same page.

Use institution or company logos only for verified relationships. Never imply technology transfer, endorsement, or affiliation through proximity or connecting lines. Use either the headline or one directional end-state verdict; do not repeat the same conclusion in a large bottom banner.

## Capital-progression

Show dates, milestones, valuation or capital events, and reasons for re-rating as one causal path. Use rising steps or an upward line only when values genuinely increase. Separate historical facts from future targets.

For a growth-path benchmark pair:

- allocate roughly 70–75% of the slide to the external precedent;
- map the project path in a 20–25% side panel;
- reserve a bottom verdict band for the investment conclusion;
- distinguish current facts from future stages or FA scenarios in the side panel;
- use the precedent to prove the terminal-value shape, never to imply that the project already has the benchmark company's scale.

Keep each stage to one event, one reason for re-rating, and one status. Do not attach full product, market, financing, and organization checklists to every stage.

## Capability-structure

Let names, roles, credentials, modules, and complementary coverage carry the page. Use 3–4 matrix columns, paired panels, or capability bands. Give each person or module one role, one unique credential, and one capability contribution. Keep portraits and product illustrations optional and subordinate. Never replace substantive credentials with large imagery or reproduce full biographies.

## Causal-mechanism

Use a 3–5 node diagram only to show a real sequence, dependency, feedback loop, or value transfer. Label causes and outcomes rather than nouns alone. Use arrows only when they communicate that logic. Remove component inventories that do not alter the mechanism. Avoid generic single-loop flywheels and decorative flow graphics.

## Visual-metaphor

Select one primary metaphor or a coordinated set of related symbols according to the page task. Make their relationship and hierarchy explicit. Keep text and evidence readable, and scale atmosphere so it supports rather than replaces the conclusion.
